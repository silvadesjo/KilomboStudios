About

Kilombo Studios is a community photography and creative space rooted in accessibility, self-expression and visual sovereignty, built for the artists, migrants and storytellers who needed a space like this to exist.

Our story

“Kilombo refers to the sovereign African communities founded by freedom seekers: spaces of refuge, self-determination and collective creativity. This studio carries that spirit forward.”
Joyce, founder

Joyce, a multidisciplinary artist, went looking for affordable studio space in Toronto and decided to build it herself instead.

Before it was a studio, 1282 Dundas St. W was an immigration consultancy: for many people, a first point of contact with a new life in Canada. Kilombo honours that history. It's still a place where people with ties to migration can arrive, create and be seen.

Open to everyone, and built especially for migrant, Indigenous, Black and racialized artists.

What we stand for

  • Accessibility

    Professional tools at prices that don't exclude the people who need them most.

  • Visual sovereignty

    Your story, told in your own voice, with resources that match your vision.

  • Community

    A space that belongs to its people, not just a venue they pass through.

  • Access for all

    Sliding-scale workshop spots, a barter and exchange program, and youth pricing for people under 25.
